# Decision log

## Closed for the hackathon MVP

- SeedLend remains the project name, provisionally.
- The immediate objective is to maximize the probability of winning a BUIDL CTC prize.
- The product is directed investment microcredit, not a cash consumption loan.
- Creditcoin holds loan state; Sepolia holds the demonstration position.
- Attestcoin verification must gate loan activation.
- The MVP ends at release eligibility, not automatic cross-chain release.
- Payment history means history inside SeedLend, not automatic Credal integration.
- The demo asset must never be represented as a legally backed RWA unless that backing is separately verified.
- Secondary-market and community effects remain hypotheses outside the main demo.
- `termsHash` commits `loanId` and all material loan terms so an otherwise identical position cannot be silently reused for a different loan.
- `SeedLendVault` rejects fee-on-transfer assets because the verified position amount must equal the quantity actually received.
- The production contracts expose no manual activation or release shortcut; test-only hooks exist only in the Foundry harness.
- The verified source evidence is stored as Attestcoin's replay-protected `queryId`; the Sepolia transaction hash remains worker/UI metadata because it is not directly authenticated as a field by the destination contract.
- T05 follows the official one-shot proof flow using `@gluwa/usc-sdk@0.18.0`; T06 migrates the decoder dependency to the current `@gluwa/asc-contracts@0.2.1` package.
- The hackathon demo asset is a fixed-supply, unbacked ERC20 used only on Sepolia and explicitly makes no legal RWA claim.
- Deployment reuses Creditcoin's official pre-deployed `EvmV1Decoder` instead of spending another testnet transaction to deploy the same library.
- Hackathon repayments use native tCTC on Creditcoin through `repay(uint256 loanId) payable`; each successful payment is recorded and forwarded immediately to the `originator`.
- The demo uses 100 tCTC principal, 108 tCTC total due and three illustrative payments of 36 tCTC. These values do not create an on-chain calendar, APR or mandatory installment size.

## Product and narrative direction — 10 September 2026

- The human problem comes before the cross-chain architecture: SeedLend should be presented as a gateway to a young person's first investment when they do not yet have the full initial capital.
- Approved homepage direction: **“Your first investment shouldn’t have to wait until you have the capital.”**
- Approved supporting direction: SeedLend helps young people build their first investment position over time by combining directed credit, financial education and verifiable on-chain ownership.
- Approved “Why this matters” direction: **“Start investing before you have the full capital.”** The explanation should connect small regular payments, investing education, risk and long-term compounding.
- Product education should be practical and gamified rather than a passive course. The working progression is `Learn → Simulate → Unlock → Invest → Build`, including interactive simulators/calculators and progressively unlocked investment choices after demonstrating understanding.
- The product may ultimately let users choose among investment categories after education. Candidate categories discussed include BTC, equities or tokenized equities, diversified assets and DeFi strategies. No production asset menu or provider is selected yet.
- Potential return is expected to be a major adoption motivator and must not be hidden behind an education-only narrative. However, SeedLend must not promise a fixed return generated by SeedLend itself. Risk, potential return and financing cost should be transparent and attributable to the financed asset or strategy.
- The hackathon demo's 100 tCTC principal / 108 tCTC total due must never be described as an 8% annual rate. It is an illustrative demo term without an on-chain repayment calendar or APR.
- The social/network-effect hypothesis should focus on the visible milestone of having a first investment and building it over time, not assume that education alone creates referrals. This remains a hypothesis to validate with real users.
- A second user asset is now part of the product thesis: **verifiable repayment history**. The current Creditcoin contract already records borrower-linked payments, amounts and timestamps and ends in `Paid`; these records can be surfaced as verifiable repayment history.
- Do not call the current data a formal bureau credit history, credit score or “on-time payment history”: the MVP has no identity layer, bureau integration or mandatory on-chain due-date calendar.
- A minimal future-facing **SeedLend Credit Passport** is a candidate near-term feature that can be derived from existing on-chain data without contract redeployment: borrower wallet, completed loans, total repaid, payment records, final status and explorer evidence.
- The next public-site revision should include the approved first-investment framing, generic repayment language rather than implying exactly three payments, and the verifiable-repayment-history benefit. The Attestcoin and public-evidence sections remain technically strong and should be preserved.

## Field observations and hypotheses to validate

These points are intentionally recorded as founder observations or hypotheses, not population statistics:

- The founder has repeatedly observed in university/student environments that small disposable amounts are often spent or wagered rather than saved or invested, with sports-betting behavior especially visible among young people.
- A key hypothesis is that many young people do have recurring small amounts such as roughly USD 5–10, but do not perceive those amounts as enough to begin investing.
- A key hypothesis is that many students substantially overestimate the minimum capital required for a first investment, potentially naming hundreds or thousands of dollars.
- A key hypothesis is that practical financial education is scarce or absent in school/university and that many young people do not understand compound interest, risk or the benefit of starting early.
- A key hypothesis is that expected return strongly influences adoption and that users may accept materially more risk when upside appears large, as seen in betting and speculative crypto behavior.
- Network effects should be tested through past behavior — for example whether users adopted betting, crypto, finance or investment apps after seeing friends use them — rather than hypothetical “would you recommend?” questions.

## Near-term customer discovery

- Run at least 10 in-person interviews; 15–20 is preferable if feasible.
- Seek at least 30 online questionnaire responses; 50–100 would provide stronger early pattern detection, while still not being presented as statistically representative.
- Compare in-person and anonymous online answers separately.
- Measure behavior before explaining SeedLend: betting usage/frequency, typical amounts, existing savings/investments, perceived minimum capital to invest, practical financial education, use of an extra USD 10, perceived cost of professional investment education, willingness to finish a short free course and expected annual return.
- Record memorable verbatim responses when participants consent; video interviews are useful only with explicit permission to record and separate permission for public reuse.

## Open and intentionally not assumed

- production repayment currency and economic terms;
- target borrower APR, cost of capital, SeedLend spread/fee and sustainable net user return;
- production asset menu and the exact education/unlock rules for higher-risk assets;
- whether social sharing/referrals create a material network effect;
- formal identity, credit scoring, credit-bureau interoperability or portable reputation model;
- final submission sector/track;
- production jurisdiction, custody, underwriting and compliance model;
- open-source license.
